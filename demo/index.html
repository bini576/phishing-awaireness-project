🚩 Red Flags You Might Have Missed:

Wrong Domain

Real: studentportal.bdu.edu.et
Fake: studentportal.bdu-login.com — a hyphen and different TLD!

SSL Certificate

Phishing sites often have valid SSL (the padlock) — it only means the connection is encrypted, NOT that the site is legitimate.

Suspicious Link Source

Phishing links often come via email, SMS, or social media pretending to be official communications.

Slight Visual Differences

Fonts, logo quality, spacing may differ slightly from the original. Attackers clone but rarely perfectly match.

No Working Links

Footer links like "About", "Contact" typically don't work on phishing pages — they're just static copies.

Urgency Tactics

"Your account will be suspended!" — attackers create panic to make you act without thinking.

🧠 Phishing Detection Quiz

Test your ability to identify phishing attacks. 6 real-world scenarios.

QUESTION 1 / 6
You receive an email from support@paypa1.com asking you to verify your account urgently. What is this?
A A legitimate PayPal security alert
B A phishing email — 'paypa1.com' uses number 1 instead of letter L
C A spam email, but harmless
D Can't tell without seeing the email body
✅ Correct! This is a classic typosquatting attack. The domain uses the number "1" (one) instead of the letter "l" (el) in "paypal". Always check the sender domain character by character for suspicious substitutions.
QUESTION 2 / 6
A website has a padlock icon (🔒) in the browser address bar. This means:
A The website is 100% safe and legitimate
B The website is owned by a trusted organization
C Your connection to the site is encrypted, but the site could still be malicious
D The site has been verified by the government
✅ Correct! HTTPS/SSL only means the connection between you and the server is encrypted. It says nothing about whether the server belongs to a legitimate organization. Phishing sites routinely use SSL certificates — they're free and easy to get.
QUESTION 3 / 6
You get a text message: "Your BDU account will be suspended in 24 hours. Click here: http://bit.ly/bdu-login". What do you do?
A Click the link immediately — you can't risk losing your account
B Click the link but don't enter your password
C Ignore it, go directly to the official site by typing the URL yourself
D Forward it to friends to warn them
✅ Correct! Never click shortened URLs in unsolicited messages. The urgency tactic ("24 hours") is a classic phishing pressure technique. Always navigate directly to the official site by typing the address yourself, or use a bookmark you trust.
QUESTION 4 / 6
Which URL is the REAL Bahir Dar University student portal?
A studentportal.bdu-edu.com
B www.bdu-studentportal.net
C studentportal.bdu.edu.et
D login.bdu.study.et
✅ Correct! The real portal is on the official .bdu.edu.et domain. Ethiopia's academic institutions use .edu.et. Any other variation (.com, .net, hyphens, extra words) is a phishing site.
QUESTION 5 / 6
Your friend sends you a Facebook message: "Check this out, I think you're in this photo: [link]". What is the most likely scenario?
A Your friend actually found a photo of you
B Your friend's account was hacked and is spreading phishing links
C It's a news article tagging you
D Safe to click since it's from a friend
✅ Correct! Social media phishing often hijacks compromised accounts to send credible-seeming messages. The "photo of you" trick is very common. If a message feels odd, verify with your friend through another channel (call or text) before clicking.
QUESTION 6 / 6
Which of the following is the BEST protection against phishing attacks?
A Using a strong, long password
B Having antivirus software installed
C Enabling Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on all accounts
D Never using public WiFi
✅ Correct! MFA (also called 2FA) is the single most effective protection. Even if a phisher steals your password, they can't log in without your second factor. Enable MFA on email, banking, social media, and university accounts immediately.

🛡️ KEY PROTECTION TIPS

Always verify the full URL — including subdomain and TLD
Enable MFA on every important account
Never click shortened or unexpected links
HTTPS ≠ safe — always check the domain too
When in doubt, navigate directly — don't click

🛡️ How to Protect Yourself

Essential practices every internet user should follow to stay safe from phishing.

🔗

VERIFY URLS

Always check the full domain. Look for typos, hyphens, or wrong TLDs (.com instead of .edu.et). Hover over links before clicking.

📱

ENABLE MFA / 2FA

Multi-factor authentication blocks 99.9% of account takeover attempts, even if your password is stolen.

🔒

DON'T TRUST HTTPS ALONE

The padlock only means encrypted connection — not a safe site. Phishing sites commonly use SSL certificates.

📧

CHECK SENDER CAREFULLY

Look at the full email address, not just the display name. support@paypa1.com ≠ support@paypal.com.

⏰

IGNORE URGENCY

Legitimate services don't demand immediate action under threat. Urgency = manipulation tactic.

📌

BOOKMARK IMPORTANT SITES

Save your bank, university, and email login pages as bookmarks. Never search for them and click random results.

🔑

USE A PASSWORD MANAGER

Password managers autofill only on the correct domain — they won't fill in a phishing site, alerting you automatically.

📚

KEEP EDUCATING OTHERS

Share this demo with friends, family, and classmates. Awareness is the best defense.
